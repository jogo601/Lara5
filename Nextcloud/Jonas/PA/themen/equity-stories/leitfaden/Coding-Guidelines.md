Coding Guidelines
General Coding Rules
Selection criterion
Coded are all self-contained statements through which a company justifies or substantiates its investment attractiveness, in particular statements on value drivers, competitive advantages, strategic capabilities, and expected investment outcomes. Not coded are contact information, legal notices and disclaimers, purely organizational information, and any content without reference to the investment story.
Coding unit
The coding unit is the smallest self-contained statement conveying exactly one investment argument. It may be a sentence, a bullet point, a paragraph, or a heading combined with its caption. Before finalizing a coding unit, the following test should be done: if removing the unit removes exactly one investment argument, the coding unit was valid.
Where a single sentence contains two substantively independent arguments (e.g. "strong balance sheet and experienced management team"), it should be split into two coding units.
Grouping ≠ unit: Shared presentation (paragraph, bullet list, heading+labels, section) does not replace the split test. Each substantively independent statement is its own coding unit.
Context unit
The context unit is the complete equity story of a company, including all documents selected under the material inclusion criteria (website, investor presentation, roadshow materials, or a combination thereof).
Evaluation unit
The evaluation unit is likewise the complete equity story of a company. Coding results are aggregated at company level in one row of the Excel database. The source document type (website / presentation / factbook) is recorded in a separate field for each coded statement.
Multiple coding
Where a single investment argument simultaneously satisfies the definitions of two (sub)categories, both are coded. This case is not resolved by choosing only one subcategory or by using residual category 99. Multiple coding is implemented through one binary column (0/1) per subcategory.
If more than two subcategories appear to apply, first apply the split test. If the unit remains inseparable, code the two best-fitting subcategories and record any additional candidate subcategory in the free-text note for review and mark it as uncertain.
Graphics and visual material
Only textual content is coded: headings, bullet labels, captions, and labels within figures. Where a figure conveys a relevant argument that was not argued textually in the vicinity of the chart, it is recorded in the free-text note field.
Language
Material is coded in its original language. Category assignment is based on substantive meaning, not on the presence of specific terms. Anchor examples are documented in the original language.
Residual category
Statements that meet the selection criterion but cannot be assigned to any existing subcategory are coded under the residual category "99 – Other / Not classifiable" and recorded verbatim in the free-text note field. The residual category is reviewed at regular intervals; if recurring patterns emerge, the category system is extended accordingly.
Documentation
For each coded equity story, the following are recorded: company, source (URL), source type, access date, language, and document format.
Business Model
Definition: Statements highlighting structural characteristics of how the company conducts its business and generates revenues, particularly regarding the composition, revenue structure, or scalability of its business model.
Subcategory 1.1
Diversified Business Model
Definition & Inclusion Criteria
Statements explicitly presenting diversification across multiple business activities, revenue sources, customer groups, markets, or other structural dimensions as a characteristic of the company’s business model.
Exclusion Rule
Statements referring to diversification primarily as a means of reducing risk or increasing resilience are coded under “Diversification & Concentration Risk Mitigation” (10.3). Statements referring specifically to the diversification or restructuring of the company’s portfolio through acquisitions, divestments, or other portfolio measures are coded under “Portfolio Strategy” (8).
Anchor Example
“Diversified international insurance group with balanced earnings contribution from primary insurance and reinsurance”, “Talanx is highly diversified in primary insurance across segments and regions.” (Talanx)
"Shell's Integrated Business Model" (Shell)
Subcategory 1.2
Recurring & Contracted Revenue Model
Definition & Inclusion Criteria
Statements highlighting a revenue model characterized by recurring, subscription-based, or contractually secured revenues, including long-term contracts, leases, or other contractual arrangements that generate ongoing or predefined revenue streams. This also includes indexed revenues when they form part of such contractual revenue arrangements.
Exclusion Rule
Statements focusing exclusively on the resulting stability, predictability, or resilience of revenues or earnings without describing the underlying recurring or contractual revenue structure are coded under “Risk & Resilience” (10.2).
Anchor Example
"Attractive business model with high share of recurring revenue" (Sartorius)
"Increasing recurring revenues through subscription and SaaS models" (Nemetschek)
Subcategory 1.3
Scalable Business Model
Definition & Inclusion Criteria
Statements highlighting the company’s ability to expand its business or revenues without a proportional increase in costs, resources, or operational complexity. This includes statements explicitly describing the business model, platform, or operating structure as scalable, or emphasizing the ability to accommodate additional customers, volumes, or revenues with limited incremental costs or resources.
Exclusion Rule
Statements referring exclusively to planned business expansion, capacity, additions, or general growth ambitions without indicating scalability of the underlying business model are coded under “Growth Strategy” (7).
Anchor Example
“By sharing our infrastructure and capabilities, our modular B2B operating system helps partners build and scale their digital business.” (Zalando)
“Our asset-light business model enables scalability which combined with high productivity is driving industry-leading margins, double-digit EPS growth and strong cash flow generation.” (DSV)
Market Position & Competitive Advantage
Definition: Statements highlighting the company’s relative position in its market or industry, or characteristics that differentiate it from competitors or provide a competitive advantage.
Subcategory 2.1
Market Leadership & Position
Definition & Inclusion Criteria
Statements indicating that the company holds a leading, strong, or established position within a market, industry, segment, or geographic area. This includes explicit claims of market or industry leadership, leading provider status, or statements demonstrating a leading position through market share or market ranking.
Exclusion Rule
Statements referring only to the attractiveness, size, or growth of a market without describing the company’s own position.
Anchor Example
“With our Automotive segment, we are the global market leader in exhaust gas recirculation systems, solenoid valves and electric pumps.” (Rheinmetall)
“We are the largest supplier of land-based systems for armed forces in Europe.” (Rheinmetall)
“Leading positions in attractive and resilient markets with low volatility and steady growth” (GEA)
“We are the leading global supplier of fresh produce with a diverse geographic footprint and leadership positions across multiple attractive product categories.” (Dole plc)
"We have built the largest community in the market." (Dassault Systèmes)
“Unilever is a €50 billion+ business with category-leading brands in every major market. Our scale is global, our execution deeply local — operating in more than 190 countries with #1 or #2 positions across 80% of our portfolio.” (Unilever)
“DSV is a world-leading transport and logistics provider [...]” (DSV)
"We operate in growing markets, where we hold strong positions with good local scale." (Vodafone)
"Market leader in logistics with diversified portfolio" (DHL)
"Fresenius Helios is Europe’s leading private hospital operator (Helios Germany & Quirónsalud Spain)." (Fresenius)
Subcategory 2.2
Competitive Advantage & Differentiation
Definition & Inclusion Criteria
Statements highlighting specific characteristics, capabilities, or forms of differentiation that are explicitly presented as distinguishing the company from competitors or providing a competitive advantage. This includes differentiation through a premium or niche focus.
Exclusion Rule
Claims referring exclusively to a leading or strong market position without specifying a source of differentiation or competitive advantage.
Anchor Example
“We have the deepest dataset in fashion & lifestyle - giving us the ultimate AI advantage as we unlock its power on our platform and beyond.” (Zalando)
“Focus on property/casualty business as a decisive competitive advantage.” (Talanx)
"The Verbund concept is one of BASF’s key strengths." (BASF)
Subcategory 2.3
Brand Strength
Definition & Inclusion Criteria
Statements highlighting the strength, recognition, reputation, or heritage of the company’s brand or brand portfolio, including well-known or established local and regional brands.

Exclusion Rule
Statements in which the brand is presented as one element
supporting a broader competitive advantage argument, rather than as the

Differentiation" (2.2). Statements emphasizing brand-driven customer
loyalty or retention are coded under "Customer Relationships &
Retention" (3.2).
Anchor Example
“The DOLE brand is the most recognized and trusted brand in fresh fruit in the United States...” (Dole plc)
"Powerful, trusted brands – 33 billionaire brands." (Nestlé)
"Powerful, desirable brands that lead in their categories and drive growth across markets." (Unilever)
Subcategory 2.4
Strategic Assets & Infrastructure
Definition & Inclusion Criteria
Statements highlighting the quality, strategic value, or advantageous location of the company’s physical assets, asset portfolio, infrastructure, facilities, production sites, or physical network as a source of competitive strength or investment attractiveness.
Exclusion Rule
Statements merely describing the existence, number, or location of assets, facilities, or production sites without presenting them as a strategic strength or investment argument.
Anchor Example
“Valuable and strategic asset base” (Dole plc)
“Extensive infrastructure and strategic asset base provides superior control over the supply chain” (Dole plc)
“Unique Global Infrastructure – Extensive network across 220 countries and territories creates high barriers to entry” (DHL)
“OUR WELL-BALANCED WORLDWIDE FOOTPRINT COMPRISES >30 PRODUCTION SITES ON FIVE CONTINENTS, ALIGNING PRODUCTION WITH MARKET DEMAND.” (BMW)
Customer & Commercial Strength
Definition: Statements highlighting the company’s customer orientation, the strength and quality of its customer relationships and customer base, or its ability to create additional value for customers through tailored or value-added solutions.
Subcategory 3.1
Customer Focus & Proximity
Definition & Inclusion Criteria
Statements highlighting a strong orientation toward customer needs and requirements or emphasizing close geographic, organizational, or operational proximity to customers, including a strong focus on understanding and addressing customer needs.
Anchor Example
“Using our shared data and infrastructure, our multiple apps serve distinct customer needs to increase our reach.” (Zalando)
Subcategory 3.2
Customer Relationships & Retention
Definition & Inclusion Criteria
Statements highlighting the strength, quality, or longevity of customer relationships and the company’s ability to maintain and retain customers over time. This includes long-term, trusted, or close customer relationships, strategic customer partnerships, high customer loyalty or retention, or strong integration into customers’ workflows or business processes that support lasting customer relationships.
PA calibration (2026-10-17): External trust awards / “most trustworthy”-type recognition → multiple coding **2.3 + 3.2** (brand/reputation and customer trust).
Anchor Example
“Building lasting habits: How our loyalty program turns shoppers into regulars.” (Zalando)
Subcategory 3.3
Customized & Value-Added Solutions
Definition & Inclusion Criteria
Statements highlighting the company’s ability to provide customized, tailored, or value-added products, services, or solutions that are adapted to individual customer requirements or extend beyond the company’s standard or core offering.
Anchor Example
“Supporting personalised Sports- and Beauty-advice through our Zalando Assistant” (Zalando)
“More personalised style advice through Zalando Assistant” (Zalando)
Subcategory 3.4
Customer Base Strength and Diversification
Definition & Inclusion Criteria
Statements highlighting the strength, breadth, quality, or diversification of the company’s customer base, including a broad or diversified customer base, a strong or high-quality customer portfolio, or the presence of established, reputable, or strategically important customers.
Exclusion Rule
Statements focusing primarily on the risk-reducing or stabilizing effects of customer diversification rather than on the customer base itself are coded under “Diversification & Concentration Risk Mitigation” (10.3).
Anchor Example
“Highly diversified customer base and partner of choice for food, beverage and pharmaceutical manufacturers around the world” (GEA)
“Our 8,800+ global customers include over 85% of the Fortune 500.” (ServiceNow)
People, Capabilities & Expertise
Definition: Statements highlighting the company’s human capital, specialized knowledge and capabilities, workforce and management quality, employee engagement, or corporate culture as organizational strengths.
Subcategory 4.1
Professional & Technical Expertise
Definition & Inclusion Criteria
Statements highlighting specialized professional, industry, market, customer, scientific, technical, engineering, or software development knowledge and expertise that supports the company’s business activities.
Exclusion Rule
Statements emphasizing the overall qualifications, skills, experience, or talent of the company’s workforce rather than specific areas of specialized expertise are coded under “Skilled Workforce & Talent” (4.2).
Anchor Example
“Deep scientific, application and regulatory expertise” (Sartorius)
Subcategory 4.2
Skilled Workforce & Talent
Definition & Inclusion Criteria
Statements highlighting the qualifications, skills, experience, or talent of the company’s workforce as an organizational strength, including references to a highly qualified, skilled, experienced, or talented workforce.
Exclusion Rule
Statements referring specifically to the quality, experience, capabilities, continuity, or track record of the company’s management or leadership team are coded under “Management Quality & Experience” (4.5).
Anchor Example
Subcategory 4.3
Employee Engagement & Retention
Definition & Inclusion Criteria
Statements highlighting employees’ engagement, commitment, or attachment to the company, as well as the company’s ability to retain its workforce and key talent, including high employee engagement, low employee turnover, or successful employee and talent retention.
Anchor Example
Subcategory 4.4
Corporate Culture
Definition & Inclusion Criteria
Statements highlighting distinctive values, norms, behaviors, or ways of working and collaborating that characterize the company’s organizational or corporate culture.
Anchor Example
Subcategory 4.5
Management Quality & Experience
Definition & Inclusion Criteria
Statements highlighting the experience, quality, continuity, capabilities, or proven track record of the company’s management or leadership team as a strength of the company.
Exclusion Rule
Statements referring to the skills, expertise, or quality of the broader workforce are coded under “Skilled Workforce & Talent” (4.2). Statements emphasizing the company’s ability to successfully implement strategies, projects, or operational initiatives are coded under “Operational & Execution Excellence” (6.1).
Anchor Example
“Highly experienced management team supported by a global base of c. 32,000 employees across 29 countries.” (Dole plc)
“Management with deep industry and company knowledge.” (Nestlé)
“Diverse, bold and high-performing leaders delivering focus, accountability and results.” (Unilever)
Innovation & Technology
Definition: Statements highlighting the company’s innovation activities, technological capabilities, development of new or improved products and services, digitalization and automation, or research and development activities.
Subcategory 5.1
Product & Service Innovation
Definition & Inclusion Criteria
Statements highlighting the development or introduction of new or significantly improved products, services, or solutions through innovation, including digital or technology-based offerings.
Anchor Example
"THE NEUE KLASSE, WITH ITS 6TH GENERATION POWERTRAIN & BATTERY TECHNOLOGIES - MAKES A QUANTUM LEAP FOR OUR CUSTOMERS." (BMW)
Subcategory 5.2
Digitalization & Automation
Definition & Inclusion Criteria
Statements highlighting the use of digital technologies or automation to transform, improve, or streamline the company’s internal processes, operations, or ways of working. This includes digital transformation, process digitalization, automation, or the use of technologies such as artificial intelligence, machine learning, or software to improve or automate internal processes and operations.
Exclusion Rule
Digital or technology-based products, services, or solutions primarily developed for customers are coded under the subcategory “Product & Service Innovation” (5.1).
Anchor Example
“Scaling AI-powered robots for efficiency gains in logistics” (Zalando)
“Fulfilment: cost efficiencies will be supported by increasing utilization and AI-based automation, further support from 2026 network optimization with additional double-digit million in cost savings from 2028 onwards.” (Zalando, also 6.2)
"BMW iFACTORY INTEGRATES FLEXIBLE PRODUCTION, SUSTAINABLE INNOVATION, ADVANCED DIGITALISATION, AND PEOPLE AT THE CORE OF TRANSFORMATION." (BMW)
Subcategory 5.3
Technology Leadership
Definition & Inclusion Criteria
Statements highlighting the company’s leading, advanced, pioneering, or superior technological position within its industry, market, or field of activity, including leadership in the development or application of technologies.
Exclusion Rule
Claims referring to a leading market or industry position without specifically referring to technological leadership are coded under “Market Leadership & Position” (2.1.)
Anchor Example
“We are the technology leader with the majority of our products.” (Rheinmetall)
"The industry-trusted IP generation & management company." (Dassault Systèmes)
"Aiming for leadership in generative AI in the AEC/O and Media industries" (Nemetschek)
Subcategory 5.4
Research & Development
Definition & Inclusion Criteria
Statements highlighting research and development activities, capabilities, programs or investments as a basis for innovation, technological advancement, or the development of new and improved products, services, or technologies.
Anchor Example
“To promote our innovative strength and maintain our technological leadership, we invest around five percent of our annual sales in R&D.” (Rheinmetall)
Operational Excellence
Definition: Statements highlighting the company’s ability to operate effectively and efficiently through strong operational processes, disciplined execution, and efficient use of resources.
Subcategory 6.1
Operational & Execution Excellence
Definition & Inclusion Criteria
Statements highlighting the company’s ability to operate through high-quality, standardized, simplified, or continuously optimized processes and to effectively and reliably implement strategic or operational initiatives. This includes statements emphasizing process excellence or optimization, continuous improvement, execution capabilities, execution discipline, or the effective implementation of measures, projects, and initiatives.
Anchor Example
"World-class execution that turns brand desirability into measurable growth." (Unilever)
Subcategory 6.2
Cost & Operational Efficiency
Definition & Inclusion Criteria
Statements highlighting the company’s ability to use resources efficiently and maintain a cost-efficient operating structure. This includes statements emphasizing cost efficiency, efficient resource utilization, productivity improvements, or operational measures aimed at reducing costs or increasing efficiency.
Exclusion Rule
Statements referring exclusively to improved margins or
profitability without describing an underlying operational efficiency or
cost-related mechanism are coded under "Achieved Profitability & Capital
Efficiency" (12.3) if already achieved, or under "Targeted Profitability
& Margins" (13.2) if targeted or expected.
Anchor Example
“Fulfilment: cost efficiencies will be supported by increasing utilization and AI-based automation, further support from 2026 network optimization with additional double-digit million in cost savings from 2028 onwards.” (Zalando, also 5.2)
“Cost leadership in more than 90% of the portfolio”, “As a result, Talanx has better management and total expense ratios than its competitors and at the same time achieves good margins.” (Talanx)
"This saves raw materials and energy, avoids emissions, lowers logistics costs and leverages synergies." (BASF)
Growth Strategy
Definition: Statements highlighting strategies, initiatives, or measures through which the company aims to achieve organic growth by expanding its existing business activities, without relying on acquisitions or other inorganic portfolio measures.
Subcategory 7.1
Geographic Expansion
Definition & Inclusion Criteria
Statements highlighting organic growth through entry into new countries or regions, international expansion, or the further development of the company’s presence and activities within existing geographic markets.
Exclusion Rule
Geographic expansion achieved primarily through acquisitions or other inorganic portfolio measures is coded under “Portfolio Strategy” (8).
Anchor Example
“In 2026, we are proud to partner with Levi’s® for their global DTC business, expanding our B2B software offering beyond Europe.” (Zalando)
Subcategory 7.2
Product & Offering Expansion
Definition & Inclusion Criteria
Statements highlighting organic growth through the expansion or extension of the company’s existing product, service, or solution offering. This includes emphasizing the introduction of additional product or service categories, expansion into adjacent offerings, broadening of the existing offering, or expansion into higher-value or premium segments as a means of organic growth.
Exclusion Rule
Statements focusing on the development or innovative characteristics of new or improved products and services without presenting them as a means of business growth are coded under “Product & Service Innovation” (5.1).
Anchor Example
“With regard to e-mobility for passenger cars, commercial vehicles and micro-mobility, we have systematically expanded and already successfully commercialized our product range.” (Rheinmetall)
“We are growing our share of customer spending by offering tailored apps for more lifestyle needs.” (Zalando)
"We have unique scale and capabilities, and are expanding our portfolio of products and services into growth areas such as unified communications, cloud and security, and IoT." (Vodafone)
Subcategory 7.3
Customer & Market Expansion
Definition & Inclusion Criteria
Statements highlighting organic growth through the acquisition or targeting of new customer groups or entry into new market segments or application areas.
Exclusion Rule
Expansion defined primarily by entry into new countries or geographic regions is coded under the subcategory “Geographic Expansion” (7.1).
Anchor Example
“Future Expansion Opportunities: General Merchandise; SMB” (Zalando, p.26, graphic)
Subcategory 7.4
Capacity & Scale Expansion
Definition & Inclusion Criteria
Statements highlighting organic growth through additions to or expansion of the company’s operational, production, service, or other business capacities, including new or expanded facilities or other internally developed capacity increases intended to support organic growth.
Exclusion Rule
Statements highlighting the quality, strategic value, or advantageous location of existing assets, infrastructure, facilities, production sites, or physical networks without presenting their expansion as a means of organic growth are coded under “Strategic Assets & Infrastructure” (2.4).
Anchor Example
Portfolio Strategy
Definition: Statements highlighting the strategic development, restructuring, or management of the company’s business portfolio through acquisitions, divestments, or other inorganic or structural portfolio measures.
Subcategory 8.1
Acquisitions & M&A
Definition & Inclusion Criteria
Statements highlighting completed or planned acquisitions, mergers, strategic takeovers, or other M&A activities as a means of expanding, strengthening, or strategically developing the company’s business portfolio or market presence, including buy-and-build activities.
Exclusion Rule
Growth initiatives based on the organic expansion of the company’s existing business activities without acquisitions or other M&A measures are coded under “Growth Strategy” (7).
Anchor Example
“DSV is a world-leading transport and logistics provider with a unique M&A track-record [...]” (DSV)
"In the context of portfolio management, we want to further strengthen our core businesses through value-adding acquisitions." (BASF)
"The Group has been annually growing by double digits on average and has been regularly expanding its portfolio by acquisitions of complementary technologies." (Sartorius)
"Targeted acquisitions to expand technological expertise and market presence" (Nemetschek)
Subcategory 8.2
Divestments
Definition & Inclusion Criteria
Statements highlighting completed or planned sales, disposal, spin-off, or separation of businesses, business units, or portfolio assets as part of the company’s strategic portfolio development, including measures aimed at focusing, simplifying, or strategically reshaping the company’s portfolio.
Anchor Example
“Successfully completed sale of Fresh Vegetables division” (Dole plc)
"In the Coatings division, we completed the sale of the Brazilian decorative paints business to Sherwin-Williams [...]" (BASF)
Subcategory 8.3
Portfolio Optimization & Transformation
Definition & Inclusion Criteria
Statements highlighting the active optimization, restructuring, transformation, rebalancing, or strategic realignment of the company’s overall business portfolio, including active portfolio management aimed at increasing its strategic fit or focus.
Exclusion Rule
Individual acquisitions or divestments without an explicit reference to broader portfolio optimization, transformation, or strategic realignment are coded only under “Acquisitions & M&A” (8.1) or “Divestments” (8.2), respectively.
Anchor Example
"Through decisive focus and simplification, Unilever is concentrating resources behind its biggest growth engines to deliver higher quality performance." (Unilever)
"Growing value through a dynamic portfolio" (Shell)
"Optimize portfolio and refine operating model" (Fresenius)
Subcategory 8.4
Acquisition Integration & Synergies
Definition & Inclusion Criteria
Statements highlighting the successful or planned integration of acquired businesses and the realization of cost or revenue synergies or other benefits resulting from the combination of businesses following an acquisition or merger.
Anchor Example
“Significant synergy contributions from ABOUT YOU transaction before achieving full 100m EUR Synergies 2025 synergy potential in 2028” (Zalando, p.31, graphic)
“[...] a unique M&A track-record based on a successful integration model supported by standardised infrastructure and an experienced management team.” (DSV)
Sustainability & ESG
Definition: Statements highlighting environmental, social, or governance-related practices and commitments, as well as products or solutions that contribute to sustainability objectives.
Subcategory 9.1
Climate & Environmental Sustainability
Definition & Inclusion Criteria
Statements highlighting the company’s efforts, commitments, or achievements related to climate protection, environmental sustainability, and the responsible use of natural resources. This includes decarbonization, greenhouse gas emission reductions, climate targets, energy or resource efficiency, renewable energy use, circularity, waste reduction, or other measures aimed at reducing environmental impact. Also included are statements citing external ESG or sustainability ratings as evidence of the company’s sustainability performance or standing.
Exclusion Rule
Statements referring to external sustainability-related developments, such as the energy transition or increasing demand for low-carbon solutions, as favorable market conditions or growth opportunities are coded under “External Drivers & Structural Tailwinds” (11).
Anchor Example
“Comprehensive ESG strategy with ambitious climate targets and a clear pathway to Net Zero by 2040” (GEA)
"Nestlé is fully committed to helping accelerate the transformation toward equitable, net-zero emission and nature-positive food systems that can nourish the world." (Nestlé)
Subcategory 9.2
Social Responsibility & Employees
Definition & Inclusion Criteria
Statements highlighting the company’s social responsibility and its sustainability-related commitments and practices concerning employees, communities, or society. This includes occupational health and safety, diversity and inclusion, equal opportunities, employee well-being, human rights, community engagement, or other social responsibility initiatives.
Exclusion Rule
Statements focusing on workforce quality, skills, engagement, retention, or corporate culture without an explicit social responsibility or sustainability context are coded under “People, Capabilities & Expertise” (4).
Anchor Example
Subcategory 9.3
Governance & Business Ethics
Definition & Inclusion Criteria
Statements highlighting responsible corporate governance, ethical business conduct, compliance, integrity, or governance and control structures presented as strengths of the company. This includes board-related practices when explicitly presented as relevant to the company’s sustainability or investment case.
Exclusion Rule
Purely descriptive or legally required information on board
composition, committees, ownership, or shareholder structures without an
explicit connection to responsible governance, sustainability, or the
company's investment case is not coded.
Anchor Example
“We stand for clean business. Uniform framework conditions and clear guidelines for lawful, compliant, ethically correct and fair conduct in day-to-day business give our employees security in making decisions.” (Rheinmetall)
“ESG targets embedded in top management remuneration” (GEA)
Subcategory 9.4
Sustainable Products & Solutions
Definition & Inclusion Criteria
Statements highlighting products, services, or solutions that contribute to environmental or social sustainability objectives or enable customers to improve their sustainability performance. This includes offerings that reduce environmental impacts, support decarbonization, improve resource efficiency or circularity, or otherwise generate explicit environmental or social benefits.
Exclusion Rule
Statements focusing exclusively on the innovative or technological characteristics of products, services or solutions without emphasizing an environmental or social sustainability benefit are coded under “Product & Service Innovation” (5.1).
Anchor Example
“Enabling the decarbonization of the energy-intensive food and beverage industry” (GEA)
"Enhancing quality of life for people, family and pets with accessible, nutritious and sustainable food." (Nestlé)
"Renewable power generation, trading and optimisation of power and pipeline gas, and development of Low Carbon Options like Hydrogen and CCS." (Shell)
Risk & Resilience
Definition: Statements highlighting the company’s ability to limit or manage risks, withstand adverse conditions or fluctuations, and enhance the stability, predictability, or resilience of its business and financial performance.
Subcategory 10.1
Business & Earnings Resilience
Definition & Inclusion Criteria
Statements highlighting the structural resilience or defensiveness of the company’s business or earnings profile against economic cycles, market fluctuations, or other adverse conditions. This includes statements describing the business or earnings profile as resilient, defensive, stable, or less cyclical, or emphasizing reduced sensitivity to volatility or adverse conditions.
Anchor Example
“Leading positions in attractive and resilient markets with low volatility and steady growth” (GEA)
“Compensating for fluctuations in earnings is a key component of our business model” (Talanx)
"Resilient, dependable and forward-thinking even in volatile environments." (Nestlé)
"Equinor has strengthened its resilience and can be cash flow neutral after all investments at an oil price around 50 dollars per barrel" (Equinor)
"Resilient Business Model – Diversified group structure and broad market exposure ensures stable cash flows throughout different economic cycles" (DHL)
"Persistent macroeconomic volatility and emerging geopolitical tensions, Fresenius delivers consistent performance. This reflects the operating strength and continued execution of #FutureFresenius." (Fresenius)
"WE USE OUR POWERFUL INNOVATIONS, OUR PASSION AND OUR FINANCIAL RESILIENCE TO SHAPE THE FUTURE OF SUSTAINABLE MOBILITY." (BMW)
Subcategory 10.2
Revenue Visibility & Predictability
Definition & Inclusion Criteria
Statements highlighting the visibility or predictability of the company’s future revenues, earnings, or other financial performance, including contracted revenue backlogs, or other mechanisms that provide future visibility or predictability.
Exclusion Rule
Statements describing recurring, subscription-based, or contractually secured revenue structures without emphasizing their resulting visibility or predictability are coded under “Recurring & Contracted Revenue Model” (1.2).
Anchor Example
"We have a sustainable and predictable financial profile [...]" (Vodafone)
"Advantages include for example higher resilience and better predictability of revenues [...]" (Nemetschek)
Subcategory 10.3
Diversification & Concentration Risk Mitigation
Definition & Inclusion Criteria
Statements explicitly linking diversification across businesses, markets, geographies, customers, products, or other dimensions to reduced concentration risk, lower volatility, decreased dependence, or increased stability or resilience.
Exclusion Rule
Statements describing diversification without an explicit
risk-reducing, stabilizing, or resilience-related effect are coded under
"Diversified Business Model" (1.1), or, where they refer specifically to
the customer base, under "Customer Base Strength and Diversification"
(3.4).
Anchor Example
“Our financial profile is characterized by a combination of growth and resilience, resulting from our diversified exposure by both segment and geography and diversified growing and sourcing.” (Dole plc)
Subcategory 10.4
Active Risk Management
Definition & Inclusion Criteria
Statements highlighting the company’s active identification, management, mitigation, or control of specific risks. This includes hedging, financial risk mitigation, maturity management, liquidity or cash buffers used for risk mitigation, and formal risk management or control systems.
Anchor Example
External Drivers & Structural Tailwinds
Definition: Statements highlighting external developments, trends, or market conditions that create favorable conditions, growth opportunities, or increased demand for the company’s products, services, or business activities.
Subcategory 11.1
Technological & Societal Megatrends
Definition & Inclusion Criteria
Statements highlighting long-term external technological or societal developments, such as digitalization, cloud adoption, artificial intelligence, electrification, demographic change, urbanization, or other long-term shifts, that create favorable market conditions, increased demand, or growth opportunities for the company.
Exclusion Rule
Statements focusing on the company’s own technologies, digitalization initiatives, or innovation activities rather than on external technological or societal developments are coded under “Innovation & Technology” (5).

“More efficient and thus lower-consumption engines as well as emission reduction and electrification are market-dictating technological drivers, which we are addressing.” (Rheinmetall)
“Benefitting from global megatrends such as continuous population growth, growing middle class, sustainable solutions, food safety & quality, and essential and affordable medicines” (GEA)
“Health & Wellness mega trend driving growth” (Dole plc)
"Stepping up in a generative economy." (Dassault Systèmes)
"The demand for mobile data is growing rapidly driven by the lack of fixed broadband access and by increased smartphone penetration." (Vodafone)
"DHL Group is geared for structural GDP+ growth, driven by long-term e-commerce and outsourcing trends." (DHL)
